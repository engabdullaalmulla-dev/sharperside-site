Where it does not work

Top divisions gain +12.65% over the base rate. Second tiers and below gain +3.03%. England's League Two gains +0.89%, which is no practical edge at all.

The reason is not mysterious: lower divisions have more squad churn, thinner and noisier data, and results that are genuinely closer to a coin toss. The model does not know more about League Two than League Two knows about itself.

The higher figure is only ever quoted with its leagues attached. A site advertising +11.7% without saying which competitions produced it would be describing a different product from the one that arrives in the inbox.

Against the consensus

Where it can be checked, the model agrees with the consensus price almost everywhere — Liverpool at 65% against a de-vigged 64.5%. That agreement is reassuring rather than disappointing: a model that disagreed wildly with a liquid consensus would far more likely be broken than brilliant.

So a large gap between this model and the consensus is treated as a probable blind spot in the model, and investigated as one. It is not treated as a discovery.

Scored every day, in public

Yesterday's forecasts are settled against yesterday's results before today's are made, and the running record is part of the daily email — the days it was wrong included. A forecasting project that reports only its good weeks is not reporting.
