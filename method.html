How the analysis works

One model, fitted from match results, with every extension measured against the version without it and dropped when it earned nothing.

The ratings

Every team carries an attack and a defence rating, fitted so that the goals each side is expected to score follow from the pair of them and the division's own home advantage:

log λ = home + attack[i] − defence[j]
log μ = attack[j] − defence[i]

With the Dixon-Coles ρ correction on the four low-scoring cells, because independent Poisson goals understate 0-0 and 1-1 — the two most common results in football. Each match is weighted exp(−ξ · days_ago), a half-life of 365 days: a match a year old counts half as much as one played today.

Three things that are load-bearing

  • The gradient is analytic, verified against finite differences to 3e-7 after every change to the objective. The backtest refits several thousand times; numerical differentiation over forty-odd parameters would cost forty-three likelihood evaluations per step and the backtest would not finish.
  • There is no learned state carried between runs. The model refits from the match table in seconds. Keeping it current is a data problem, not a compute one.
  • Form is venue-specific. Home form and away form are separate readings, because a side's record at home says little about its next away trip and averaging the two hides exactly that.

What the model uses, and what it only checks

These are kept visually apart in every fixture, and the separation is the discipline rather than a formatting choice. A narrative that runs we rate them 1.43× the division straight into they always beat these opponents reads as one argument when only the first is in the number.

What it will not do

It will not name a result. It will not tell anyone what to do with a number. And where the consensus disagrees with it sharply, the gap is treated as a probable blind spot in the model rather than as a discovery — the model agrees with the consensus almost everywhere it is checked, so the exceptions are far more likely to be its own errors.
