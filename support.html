Why this needs support

The analysis is free to read and will stay free. The data underneath it is not free, and that is the whole of the ask.

What it costs to run

  • A paid data plan. Results are public domain, but fixtures, lineups, absences and player statistics are not. Every fixture that gets a proper reading — who is missing, what it costs, what the squad has actually done this season — is paid for by the request.
  • Writing the fifteen that get posted. The cards are composed from the computed facts and then verified against them before they ship; a draft that asserts something the data does not hold is refused and rewritten rather than published.
  • Running it every morning. Fifty-odd fixtures take roughly ten minutes of compute, seven days a week, plus the settling of yesterday's forecasts before today's are made.
  • Sending it properly. From info@sharperside.com on its own domain, with the authentication records that keep a daily email out of a spam folder — not from a personal mailbox.

What it does not do

  • No advertising. The reader is not the product.
  • No affiliate arrangements. There is nobody this project could send a reader to whose interests would not immediately contaminate what it says about a fixture.
  • Nothing is sold as a tip. The strongest reading is a reading. A product paid for by being right about results would have every incentive to stop publishing the fixtures it gets wrong, and this one publishes them.
  • No open or click tracking. The platform offers both. They are switched off, and the DNS record that click tracking needs was never added, so nobody here knows whether you opened this or which link you followed. The measurement that matters is whether the forecasts were right, and that is published.

What support buys

More competitions read properly, rather than more competitions read badly. The model is worth having in a top division and close to worthless in a fourth tier — the numbers are here — and the difference is mostly data. A division with thin coverage produces thin forecasts, and the fix is paid requests rather than a cleverer model.

It also buys the part nobody sees: every forecast settled against its result and kept, so that next year's version of this page can be judged against this year's claims rather than re-argued.

Support it

$15 a month, through Stripe. You get exactly what a free reader gets — the same analysis, at the same hour, with nothing held back — because a version that held something back would be a different product and this page would have to say so.

Leaving the list and cancelling the payment are two separate things, and it would be easy for this page to pretend otherwise. The removal link at the bottom of every email stops the emails; it does not reach Stripe. To stop being charged, use the payment portal — the link is in every email too, next to the other one.
